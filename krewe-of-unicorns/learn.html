Krewe Style

The Unicorn in the Krewe

What we wear on parade day
✨ Click to read the story ▾

In the Krewe of Unicorns, the only rule of dress is to shine. Some members go full mythical, in flowing capes, iridescent wings, and spiral-horn headbands. Others keep it simple with a rainbow sash and a handful of glitter. Wear whatever wonder calls to you; the only requirement is that you wear it proudly.

On parade morning you'll see every version of it rolling out together: capes beside tutus, pastel manes beside neon boas, and horn headbands catching the Florida sun. Add a sash, a good pair of comfortable shoes, and a big handful of beads, and you're parade-ready. New to all this? The lesson cards above sort the looks, and any member will happily help you find your sparkle.

Lore & Legend

Unicorn Myths & Legends

Sorting fact from folklore
✨ Click to read the story ▾

Few creatures carry as many tall tales as the unicorn. Could its horn really cure any poison? Did knights once hunt them through enchanted forests? Was every "unicorn horn" in a king's treasury the real thing? The truth is often older, and stranger, than the legend.

For a thousand years, the unicorn lived in the same books as the elephant and the crocodile: the medieval bestiaries listed it as a genuine animal, described by scholars who never doubted it walked the far edges of the map. Only later did it slip from natural history into pure enchantment, and somewhere in that crossing it became the perfect emblem for a krewe that celebrates believing in wonderful things.

From the Archives

The Great Alicorn Trade

A true tale of horns and treasure
✨ Click to read the story ▾

Here is a piece of real history stranger than any fairy tale. For centuries, spiraled "unicorn horns", known as alicorns, were among the most valuable objects on earth, worth many times their weight in gold. Kings kept them in their treasuries; apothecaries ground them into medicine believed to detect and defeat any poison.

The secret? Nearly every one of them was the long, spiraled tusk of the narwhal, the small Arctic whale sometimes called "the unicorn of the sea." Traders who knew the truth kept it well, sailing the tusks south from the far north and letting the legend do the pricing. It's a reminder that a good story has always been worth its weight in wonder, something the krewe understands completely.

💜 Krewe Life & Giving Back

Krewe Life

The Morning the Parade Begins

Parade day
✨ Click to read the story ▾

There's a particular kind of magic to parade morning. The capes come out of their garment bags, the beads get untangled, and somebody's always missing a horn headband. Then the krewe band strikes up its first bright note, glitter starts to drift through the air, and a hundred neighbors suddenly become one herd.

Watch a first-year member on that morning. They arrived early and a little nervous, fussing with an unfamiliar cape, wondering if they'd fit in. Three hours later they're waving to strangers like old friends, tossing beads to kids riding high on shoulders, and walking taller than they did at dawn. Nobody teaches that. The route does.

That's the secret we relearn every season: the parade we give Tampa Bay gives back double. Strangers on the curb wave their little rainbow flags, children dash for beads, and for a few dazzling hours the whole bay feels like family. We march to bring the joy, and we come home carrying more of it than we brought.

Krewe Life

The Family You Choose

Our welcome
✨ Click to read the story ▾

Ask almost anyone in the Krewe what kept them, and they won't say the parades or the party. They'll say the people. Folks arrive knowing one member, or none, and within a season they've got a whole table of friends saving them a seat. Believer or skeptic, glitter-covered or not, everyone's welcome.

Then something quietly wonderful happens. The shy one ends up running a committee. The one who swore they'd never dance in public is the one starting the conga line. The couple who joined "just for the parade" is hosting the planning dinner. Nobody sets out to become more here; there's simply room to try, and a whole herd cheering the moment you do.

"All for Magic, Magic for All" isn't just our motto; it's how a stranger at their first meeting becomes the one leading the parade a few years on. New faces, as we like to say, are simply old friends we haven't met yet. Pull up a chair. Yours is next.

Giving Back

More Than a Good Time

Service & charity
✨ Click to read the story ▾

For all the sparkle, the Krewe has always believed a good time should do some good. Over the years our members have given thousands of dollars and countless hours to Tampa Bay: stocking shelves at food banks, standing beside children in the hospital, and filling toy drives, coat drives, and every gap in between.

It's quiet work, mostly, far from the parade route. No beads, no crowds. Just a Saturday morning, a borrowed truck, and a family stepping into a furnished home for the first time. Ask the members who were there which day of the season they'd never trade, and that's the one they name. It works in a way that's hard to explain and easy to feel: show up to carry someone else's load, and you walk out lighter.

Every hour given is a thread of color, and the threads become the same bright rainbow that holds a krewe, and a community, together. If that sounds like your kind of morning, our Service & Charity page is the place to start. Bring a friend. Come see what a little magic can build.

🌈 Rainbows & Sky Science

3The Science of Rainbows🌈 Sky science · watch and wonderClick to learn ▾🦄

What you'll learn

  • A rainbow is light taking itself apart: sunlight bends, or refracts, as it enters a raindrop, splitting into its colors.
  • Each droplet acts like a tiny prism, bouncing the light off its back wall and sending it out at a precise angle.
  • That angle is almost exactly 42 degrees from the point directly opposite the sun, which is why a rainbow is always a perfect arc.
  • No two people ever see the same rainbow: it's built from the droplets lined up with your own eyes.

✨ Fun fact: A rainbow has no end and no edge you can reach. Walk toward it and it politely walks away, because it's an angle, not a place.

Sky Science

Double Rainbows & the Dark Band

Light doing tricks
✨ Click to read the story ▾

Sometimes, if you're lucky, a rainbow arrives with a twin. A second, fainter arc appears above the first, and here's the lovely detail: its colors run backwards, red on the inside, violet on the outside. That's because the light has bounced twice inside each raindrop instead of once, flipping the order on its way out.

Look between the two bows and you'll notice the sky there is a touch darker. That shadow has a name, Alexander's band, first described some eighteen centuries ago. No raindrops send light into that stretch of sky, so it stays dim, framed by color on both sides. Nature, it turns out, understands the art of a good backdrop as well as any krewe designer.

Star Lore

Reading the Night Sky

Finding your way by starlight
✨ Click to read the story ▾

Long before maps and lighthouses, travelers found their way by the stars. Sailors steered by Polaris, farmers timed their planting by the rising of certain constellations, and every culture told stories in the same patch of sky, seeing a great bear where another saw a plough, a hunter where another saw a lion.

The night sky is the oldest library there is, and it's free to anyone who looks up. Learn just a handful of shapes, the Big Dipper, Orion's belt, the steady North Star, and you'll never feel quite lost again. It's why we chose the star as one of our emblems: a small, faithful light that has guided wanderers home for as long as there have been wanderers to guide.

5Gemstones, Crystals & Their Folklore💎 Lore · a sparkling readClick to learn ▾🦄

What you'll learn

  • A crystal is simply matter arranged in a perfectly repeating pattern, which is why so many gems grow in flat, geometric faces.
  • Amethyst, our favorite violet stone, is quartz colored by a trace of iron and a touch of natural radiation deep in the earth.
  • Across history, people assigned meanings to stones: amethyst for calm and clarity, moonstone for new beginnings, quartz for focus.
  • These are folklore, not medicine, but the meanings make thoughtful gifts and lovely additions to a bit of krewe finery.

Gems & Folklore

A Pocket Guide to Crystal Lore

Stones and their old meanings
✨ Click to read the story ▾

People have loved and lettered meaning into stones for as long as they've dug them from the ground. Amethyst, the violet of our own krewe, has long stood for calm and clear thinking. Moonstone, with its soft inner glow, is the stone of new beginnings. Clear quartz was thought to sharpen focus, and rose quartz to open the heart.

None of it is medicine, and we'd never pretend otherwise, but there's real joy in the folklore. Choosing a stone for a friend "because it means fresh starts," or wearing amethyst to the Enchanted Ball because it's the herd's color, turns a pretty rock into a small, thoughtful message. That's magic of the everyday kind, and it's the kind we like best.

Mythical Kin

The Phoenix, the Pegasus & the Kirin

The unicorn's legendary company
✨ Click to read the story ▾

The unicorn never gallops alone. Across the world's myths it shares the sky and the forest with a whole menagerie of wonders. Pegasus, the winged white horse of Greek legend, carried heroes and, with a stamp of his hoof, struck the spring of Hippocrene from the mountainside, a fountain said to inspire poets ever after.

The phoenix lives for centuries, then builds a nest of fragrant wood, burns away, and rises new from its own ashes, the world's oldest symbol of hope after hardship. And the gentle kirin of Japan and qilin of China, chimerical, scaled, and kind, appear only in ages of peace and to people of good heart, treading so softly they harm not a single blade of grass.

Different lands, different names, one shared idea: that the world is bigger and stranger and more beautiful than it first appears. That's a belief worth marching for.

Krewe Lingo

A Few Words of Magic

The language of the herd
✨ Click to read the story ▾

A few words go a long way. Raise a glass and you'll hear Sparkle on!, our all-purpose toast, farewell, and blessing rolled into one. Greet a newcomer and it's a thousand welcomes, because one never felt like enough. And whenever there's joy to be found, someone will grin and say, "let's chase a rainbow."

The rest is delightfully invented: a nervous first-timer is a sparkling; a truly great parade is a proper shimmer; and the annual scramble to finish costumes the week of the Ball is, fondly, glitter season. None of it is in any dictionary, and that's the point. Every family builds its own little language, and stepping into a krewe's is one of the first quiet signs that you belong.

Symbols

The Spiral Horn: Symbol of Wonder

Our emblem
✨ Click to read the story ▾

The spiral horn, the alicorn, is among the oldest and best-loved of magical symbols. Traced as a single climbing line, it has no true beginning and no end, a fitting emblem for wonder, healing, and the belief that something marvelous is always just over the next hill.

You'll find it carved on old tapestries, painted into medieval manuscripts, and worked into jewelry to this day. We carry it as our quiet reminder that a krewe, like the horn, always reaches upward, and that a little wonder, shared hand to hand, is the most magical thing there is.
