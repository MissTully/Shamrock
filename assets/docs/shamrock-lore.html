The Founding Tale

The spark came in 1998, when five friends — Matt Glow, Tim Hubbell, Hayden Polk, Chris Jenkins, and Steve Hubbell — walked the Ybor City parades as security for Ye Loyal Krewe of Grace O'Malley and thought: we could march ourselves.

After the 1999 Gasparilla season they made it official. With Don Cassels as the group's first spokesperson, they petitioned the St. Patrick's Day Parade Committee — and in 1999, nineteen founders stepped onto the St. Patrick's Day parade route for the first time. By 2000 the krewe was forty strong with its first float; by 2006, one hundred strong.

The motto said everything from day one: "All for Fun, Fun for All."

Secrets in the Tartan

Every color in the krewe's cloth carries a meaning, drawn from the Gaelic Revival that gave Ireland the saffron kilt:

ColorWhat it stands for
GreenIreland's lush land and patriotism
BlueThe ancient Irish aristocracy and loyalty
GoldWarmth and prosperity
RedCourage and tenacity
BlackPower and persistence

And a quiet signature few ever notice: unlike the back-pleated Scottish standard, Irish kilts here may be side-pleated — the Irish silhouette, hiding in plain sight on every parade route.

The Castle of Shenanigans

The krewe's float didn't come from a catalog. In 2019, members built the Castle of Shenanigans themselves — weekends of coffee, lumber, and sawdust — engineering it strong enough to carry a crew of bead-throwers, light enough to tow, and low enough to clear every wire on the route.

"We didn't just build a float. It built us."
